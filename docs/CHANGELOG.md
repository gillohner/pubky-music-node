# Changelog

Newest first. Dates are when the change landed on `main`.

## 2026-10-25

- **Vault devices prove themselves** (STEWARD-VAULT.md item 3). Only a grant that names the vault itself (`/priv/pubky.music/vault/:rw`) counts: an app approved for all storage is not trusted with the sign-ins. The node asks Ring for that capability, and an older sign-in is asked to sign in again. Each client registers in `/priv/pubky.music/vault/index.json` with the pubky SDK's custom grant proof: Ring's grant, plus a proof over its vault key signed with the grant's client key. Apps never hold the identity secret.
  - Only entries the SDK's verifier accepts get keys: the owner's grant, covering the vault, for that key, and not signed out.
  - A new device is added to existing sign-ins by one that can read them (same epoch). A signed-out device can't re-register, and the next write rotates past it.
  - Vault files are written under the homeserver's WebDAV lock.
  - Tested on a real homeserver with forged, borrowed and garbage entries.
- **The node reads and writes the sources vault** (STEWARD-VAULT.md item 2, `pm_pubky::vault`).
  - The node gets its own X25519 vault key: a 0600 file, made once, equal to the Noise static key pubky-noise derives from the same secret.
  - `Vault` reads and writes a person's sign-ins over their session, encrypted to the node and to the clients the caller names.
  - Rewriting keeps the data key unless a client is dropped; then the key rotates. A sign-in the node can't open is never overwritten.
  - Tested against a real homeserver 0.16.2 in CI's bundle job: two clients share a sign-in, the homeserver holds only ciphertext, and a dropped client can't read the next write. Nothing in the UI uses it yet.
- **Downloads for phones and laptops** (STEWARD-DOWNLOADS.md): `GET /v1/library/tracks/{id}/download` serves the original, or a transcode with `?format=aac|mp3|opus&bitrate=`, and lets an interrupted download resume (ranges, `If-Range`, `ETag`).
  - The original streams from disk. Files on a share or drive, and transcodes, are made once into a capped cache.
  - Listeners may download what they may play. The homeserver's files come through the node.
  - Transcodes use ffmpeg where the node has it (501 otherwise; the original always works).
- **The sources vault, spec v1** (STEWARD-VAULT.md item 1): `crates/pubky-music-specs/VAULT.md`. Streaming sign-ins go to `/priv/pubky.music/vault/<service>.json`, encrypted on the device to every client the person authorised. The building blocks:
  - a random data key per blob, wrapped to each client's X25519 key (pubky-noise's primitives: X25519, HKDF-SHA-256, XChaCha20-Poly1305);
  - a device list in `index.json`, whose entries prove themselves with the pubky SDK's grant proofs (Ring's grant plus a custom proof over the client key; no app holds the identity secret);
  - the homeserver's WebDAV lock for refreshes, with a lease file where locks are refused, and rotation when a device signs out.
  - The `vault` feature of `pubky-music-specs` has the types and the reference encryption. `tests/vectors/vault-v1.json` holds the vectors, reproduced independently by `check_vault_v1.py`; a client proof is checked with the SDK's own verifier. CI runs the feature's tests.
- **TIDAL signs in with what another app on this machine already has** (mn-sources, STEWARD-CREDENTIALS-REUSE.md step 2): two more ways in Sources › TIDAL › Sign in. *A sign-in from another app on this machine* looks, read-only, in tidal-dl-ng's and beets' python-tidal session file, tidal-dl's token file and streamrip's config, shows what it found by user and expiry (never a token), and uses it on one click; a file picker takes the file from elsewhere. *Paste a token* takes a token file's contents or a bare access token. Imports are kept like the node's own sign-in (sealed in backups, gone with Sign out), renewed by the node where the token came from the client this build carries, and refused in plain words when expired or unreadable. Music Assistant's encrypted settings are listed, never read. `POST /v1/sources/{source}/signin/import {id|text}`; `/signin` answers carry `imports` and `places`.
- **One Sign in for TIDAL, the ways side by side** (mn-sources, STEWARD-CREDENTIALS-REUSE.md step 1): Sources › TIDAL shows one Sign in group: a short list of ways labelled by where they come from (Pubky Music's app · tidal-dl-ng's client, the device sign-in tidal-dl-ng and python-tidal use · Your own app), the recommended one preselected, one button; once signed in, one status line with Change way and Sign out. Picking tidal-dl-ng's client is what the Full tracks switch used to do; quality and health stay under it. The Sources list says per source which way is in use. `GET/PUT/DELETE /v1/sources/{source}/signin`; `/v1/sources` rows carry `sign_in`. Sign-ins imported from other apps on the machine and a pasted token follow as further ways.

  - `GET /v1/remote/tunnel` adds `restart_hint`: Umbrel and bare-metal installs say how to restart in their own words.

  - **No artist:** files with no artist get no guesses by title alone.
  - "Compare with the album" then compares against the right album.

- **Phones** (second review #22):

  - Confirm matches no longer says "left" and "right" (the sides stack on a phone).

## 2026-10-23

- **Crossfade per zone** (compare-music-assistant #11), off by default. `PUT /v1/zones/{id}/crossfade {seconds}` (up to 12 s) fades each track into the next with an equal-power curve; the next track then carries on from where the mix left it. Songs of the same album stay gapless, and there's no fade across a format change. While it fades, the signal path shows a `crossfade` stage (Enhanced), so a crossfading zone is never labelled bit-perfect.
- **Party mode** (compare-music-assistant #4). Settings › People › Party starts a party on a zone and shows a guest link and QR. Guests open it on their phones, give a name, search the library and add songs, with no account. Their songs play right after the current one, in the order they came, before the rest of the queue; an idle zone starts. Limits per guest (songs per hour, songs waiting at once) are checked on the node, and a guest token opens nothing but the party. Links expire after 1 to 72 hours, and ending a party stops its guests at once.

- **Measured loudness for files without ReplayGain tags** (compare-music-assistant #7). While a zone has loudness on, the node measures those files in the background (EBU R128, one at a time) and the loudness stage uses the result where the tag is missing: the gain to −18 LUFS, held back so the peak stays at full scale. Album mode uses the album's measured loudness once all its tracks are measured. A changed file is measured again; one that can't be decoded or is silent isn't retried. With loudness off everywhere nothing is measured and playback stays bit-perfect. `GET /v1/library/loudness` shows the progress; `POST /v1/library/loudness/measure` starts a pass.
- **BluOS players** (Bluesound, NAD; compare-music-assistant #9, `crates/pm-out-bluos`). Found over mDNS (`_musc._tcp`) and named from their `/SyncStatus`, each player is a zone: it fetches the zone's stream (`/Play?url=`, bit-perfect WAV up to 24/192) and its volume follows the zone. A zone of BluOS players only is a BluOS group: the first leads, the others follow it in sync (`/AddSlave`), and the group ends when the zone stops. Off with `[outputs] bluos = false`. Tested against a fake player that answers like the BluOS API.
- **MPD servers as zones** (compare-music-assistant #15, `crates/pm-out-mpd`). List them under `[outputs] mpd` (`host`, `host:port` or `password@host:port`); each one that answers is a zone. Over MPD's own protocol it plays the zone's stream (`addid` / `playid`, bit-perfect WAV up to 24/192), follows the zone's volume (`setvol`), and on stop removes only its own playlist entry. Passwords never show in logs.

## 2026-10-22

- **WebDAV shares are library folders** (Nextcloud, ownCloud, a NAS; owner-approved list §6.4). Settings › Library › WebDAV shares takes the folder's address and a user and password. The node scans the share like a folder, fetching only each song's tags with range requests, and plays songs from the share in any zone or the browser; nothing is copied. Shares are read again every 6 hours or with Rescan. Removing one takes its songs out of the library and leaves the files alone. The password stays in the node. In `pm-library` this adds `tags::probe_and_read_from` (the same probe on any seekable source) and `Scanner::scan_remote` (a scan over a listing), as agreed with the node agent. Tested in CI against a real WebDAV server (WsgiDAV): a wrong password refused, both songs scanned, a zone playing to the end, a byte-identical browser stream, a deleted file gone after a rescan, the share removed with its songs.
- **Radio, podcasts, Plex and Audiobookshelf respect the Sources on/off switch.** While one is off, its own routes answer 404 "… is turned off on Sources", as search and items already did.
