# Changelog

Newest first. Dates are when the change landed on `main`.

## Unreleased

### 2026-11-07

- **A stream that doesn't answer ends with an error instead of holding the zone.** A radio or web stream whose server accepts the connection but sends nothing now fails after 20 seconds, like one that stops sending mid-song. A live stream still plays for as long as it sends. Stop, Next and Play are taken while such a stream is being skipped, not after every item in the queue was tried, and the next song is opened ahead on its own thread, so a next item slow to answer never holds the zone.

- **Looking for devices no longer adds the node's own zones as speakers, and late speakers find their zone by themselves** (a rescan on the NUC made five `<zone>-2` zones that would have played into the node itself, and a start missed the Linn's UPnP AV side and the TV). The zones the node offers as UPnP/DLNA renderers are recognised by their ID or their address and never taken as outputs, typed addresses included. At start the search asks three times within its window; each UPnP renderer that had a zone is remembered at its description address and found there when the search misses it; and the node looks again 20 s after starting and every 5 minutes, so a TV or a Linn that answers late gets its zone without pressing "Look for devices". A device that answered but couldn't be described now says so in the log.

- **Leaving matches as they are: clearer Undo** (follow-ups from the reviews of "Reviewing matches is optional"). Pressing Undo says at once that the songs are coming back, and on the review page the focus returns to the page, not its body. An album's Undo says it brings back that album's skipped songs (earlier ones too). **Bring back N skipped** asks for a second press, since it undoes every "Leave as is" at once. While Identify runs, the card doesn't offer Skip all unsure (its redraws could take a press twice).

- **Seeking in a TIDAL track no longer ends it** (owner's report: dragging the seek bar on a TIDAL full track showed "decode error … out-of-bounds seek for a non-seekable stream" and the next song started). And if any stream takes a seek and then can't read, the track goes on from where it was and the player says "this stream cannot seek", instead of skipping to the next song.

- **Exclusive USB: the app plays a USB DAC with its own driver** (owner: "we need to support usb DACs with bit perfect stream"), also on phones below Android 14 and where Android refuses bit-perfect. Settings › This phone › Preferred mode: Exclusive USB. Android asks once whether the app may use the DAC; the app then takes the DAC from Android's driver, sets the DAC's clock to each song's rate and reads it back, and sends the samples untouched, paced by the DAC's own clock. Not yet tried on a phone: the next device test says whether it works.
  - **Honest labels:** "Exclusive USB · FiiO KA17 · 96 kHz / 24 bit" only when the DAC read back exactly that rate and the stream runs; otherwise "not confirmed", and never bit-perfect.
  - **Never louder:** the DAC's own volume becomes the zone's volume ("Use the DAC's volume", on by default), and it starts no louder than Android played. When the DAC goes back to Android, its volume is no higher than before. A DAC without a volume of its own plays at full level: chosen below Android's full volume, the app asks first, as for Bit-perfect.
  - **When it applies:** Auto uses it only where bit-perfect isn't available and only once access was allowed; it never asks. The DAC goes back to Android when another mode is chosen (from the next song), when it is unplugged (the zone pauses), and when the app closes while nothing plays. Meanwhile other apps' sounds play from the phone's speaker.
  - **Safer start:** Android's volume is read before the app takes the DAC. The volume used is the one on the DAC's playback path (never a microphone's), and nothing plays until the DAC has taken it. A DAC that was muted stays muted, and one that can't go as quiet as Android played starts muted. A failed attempt to take the DAC never lets the phone's speaker play. The DAC starts at Android's own level for it, read only while Android plays media to that DAC and nothing else (on Android 9 to 12 only where the phone tells that route); otherwise, for example with media on Bluetooth while the DAC is plugged in, it starts muted. Each format's own playback path decides which volume and clock are used. A path starts at Android's own level for it, or the level you set on it, never louder than the level you chose on another path, and muted when you muted; unmuting on one path unmutes the others too. A volume the DAC could route around (a selector or mixer that can bypass it) doesn't count as the path's own. A path without a volume of its own plays at full level only when Android plays the DAC itself at full volume (Bluetooth or the speaker at 100% doesn't count); otherwise only after you agree, never while the zone is muted. Given back, it is as it was before, but muted if you muted it (or it can't go as quiet as you set it) and no louder than you set it; taken again later, that same DAC keeps your mute and level, also after another USB device took its place for a while (never another DAC, not even one of the same model with the same serial number; forgotten when Android says it was unplugged). When Exclusive USB refuses a format because the zone is muted, unmuting the zone lets it play.
  - **Giving it back:** pausing keeps nothing on the DAC, so closing the app gives it back. When playing fails for good (the DAC's clock won't take the rate again, or the DAC stops taking audio), or the DAC turns out not to take a format, the DAC goes back to Android at once. After the app was force-stopped, it gives back exactly the DAC it had at its next start, never another one of the same model. "Exclusive USB" is no longer shown as confirmed once audio stops reaching the DAC, and not while paused.
  - **For now:** USB Audio Class 2 DACs such as the KA17 first; class 1 plays at full level (its volume isn't driven yet) and is untested. DSD (native or DoP) is not played this way yet.
  - **For other apps:** `PUT /v1/outputs/{id}/device` takes `mode: exclusive` and `dac_volume`.

The node stops converting to 44.1/48 kHz for the phone: a 96/24 or 192/24 file reaches the DAC as it is, with no resample and no requantize, where the DAC lists that format.
  - **Honest labels:** the Sound panel says "Bit-perfect · FiiO KA17 · 96 kHz / 24 bit" only when Android confirmed it for the format playing now and nothing in the node changes the samples. Otherwise it says why, for example "the DAC does not list 352.8 kHz for bit-perfect", "Android refused bit-perfect for this format", or "the node changes the samples (volume, DSP or a conversion)".
  - **Never louder:** Android applies no volume of its own in bit-perfect mode (the level is the DAC's). So Auto, the default, plays bit-perfect only while Android's media volume is at full, and shared otherwise, saying why. Settings › This phone › Preferred mode: Bit-perfect, chosen below full volume, first asks whether to play at full level; with OK it plays bit-perfect for that DAC, and Settings and the Sound panel keep saying so (Cancel keeps the mode). A change applies from the next song. A 96/24 or 192/24 file the DAC lists stays exactly that. On Auto, turning Android's volume down while it plays bit-perfect switches it to shared at once (a short gap, from where it was), so the volume keys work; turned down during a pause, it carries on shared. It never goes back to full level without your OK.
  - **Unplugging pauses:** unplugging the DAC mid-song, or at a song change, pauses the zone ("FiiO KA17 was unplugged: paused") instead of playing on from the phone's speaker. Android's warning before it moves audio stops the bit-perfect track at once, and nothing is written to a track Android plays anywhere but the DAC. Play carries on where it was once the DAC is back. - **The Android app updates itself** (owner's ask). Like the desktop app, it checks once at start on the channel it was built for (edge builds follow edge, releases follow the latest release) and asks "Odeion X is available (this is Y). Install it now?". After Install it downloads the APK for the phone (arm64 on most phones), installs it only when it matches the SHA-256 the release lists, and hands it to Android, which asks you to confirm and installs only an app signed with the same key. The first time, the app explains that Android needs your permission to install apps and opens that setting. A failed check (no network) says nothing. Local builds never check, and the `.aab` has no updater (an app from Google Play updates only through Play). The release feed (`latest.json`) gets an `android` entry; desktop entries stay as they were. Edge builds now show their full version (`0.3.0-412`) on Android too. Checked in CI on Android 15 and 17 emulators: a tampered download is refused, and the right one installs over the app and keeps its data.

- **Tighter request checks for a node without an API token:**
  - Such a node listens on this computer only, and now answers only at `127.0.0.1`, `localhost` or `[::1]`, from its own pages (opened as pages, not in a frame). To reach it by another name, for example through a proxy, give it an `[api] token`. The desktop and Android app, finding such a node on the network, tries it as the node on this computer first (only when the node there has the same id), and otherwise says that it needs a token.
  - Its live updates open only from its own pages, and other sites' pages reach it only by opening one of its pages (a GET; a form another site posts is refused, and so are the zones' UPnP routes).
  - The node's pages can't be shown inside another site.

- **The node's address record on your homeserver no longer holds the node's API token.**
  - The record keeps the node's name, addresses and version. The desktop and Android app finds the node through it as before, then asks you to approve a sign-in to the node itself in Pubky Ring, so each device has a sign-in of its own. Connecting a new device therefore takes two approvals in Ring, both by you: the app takes the node's sign-in only from the identity whose record named the node, approved as its owner, and refuses a node that says it belongs to someone else. A device's sign-in is kept apart from browser sessions: logging out of the page inside the app, or many browser log-ins, leave it. Make a new token ends it, and the app then signs in again by itself with one approval. A node that refuses the app's sign-in for any other reason gets the same: one approval, not Forget and two.
  - On its first start after the update, a node that had written its token there replaces that token. Apps and browsers then sign in to the node again, once.
  - A token you set in the node's config (`[api] token`, `PM_TOKEN`) can't be replaced by the node: Health asks you to change it.

- **Covers, portraits and profile pictures are served by what they are.**

## 0.2.1

### 2026-10-29

- **Edge builds say they are edge.** The node's version (`/v1/info`, Settings › About) is `0.2.1-edge+<commit>` on a build of `main` and `0.2.1` on the release; edge used to report the last release's number.
- **Back up and restore the node with its own homeserver in one command.** `deploy/bundle/backup.sh` writes a folder (yours only, the newest 7 kept, `KEEP=`) with the backup file from Settings (sign-ins locked under a passphrase it makes, or `PM_BACKUP_PASSPHRASE`), a snapshot of the library, the node's data folder and tunnel settings, the homeserver's PostgreSQL dump and data (its key and the users' files), `.env`, the compose files and the network-share volumes the node mounts (`config/recreate-volumes.sh`, with the share's address and options); never the music. `deploy/bundle/restore.sh <folder>` checks the checksums and puts it all back into an empty install, then the sign-ins through the node's own restore; it refuses a bundle that already has containers or data. CI backs up the bundle, removes it with its volumes and restores it. The backup guide covers shares, a nightly cron line, updating with `PM_TAG` and going back.
- **A backup counts every favourite.** Hearts on songs, albums and artists without a MusicBrainz id (kept on the node only) were missing from the counts a backup and its restore show ("0 favourites"); they were always restored.
- **An older node refuses a library a newer one changed.** Going back to an earlier version after an update that changed the library's database stopped working quietly; the node now stops with "this library was opened by a newer version of Pubky Music" and the guide says how to put back the library from before the update.
- **Linn DS and other OpenHome renderers play over UPnP.** They never started. The zone waited for the renderer's answer to Play before writing any audio, while a Linn answers Play only once it has read the start of the stream: both waited ~5 s, then the Linn gave up (the node sent the answer's headers and no audio). Play now goes out on its own thread and the stream has audio at once; a refused Play is the zone's error at once. Then, the node's live WAV says its sizes are `0xFFFFFFFF`, which Linn's WAV codec rounds up (odd sizes are padded) to 0, so it ended the stream before the first sample; and a Linn reads the start of a live stream, hangs up and asks again once its pipeline is ready, while the node handed each stream out once (the zone stopped on "listener went away" every ~10 s). And a Linn stops at once on a chunked stream, which is how HTTP/1.1 sends a stream of unknown length: pull streams now answer in HTTP/1.0, so the body simply runs until the connection closes. A Linn renderer now gets `live.wav`, the same WAV with RIFF size 0 (OpenHome's mark of a continuous stream), and every pull stream can be fetched again: a listener that disconnects gives it back, the next request gets it with its own header, from the first byte when the one before left within 3 s having taken under 1 MB. A zone whose listener stays away 5 s stops with "listener went away". The stream stays a live WAV of the zone's output (DSP, crossfade and gapless as before), not the file.
- **A zone's error reaches the apps.** A playback error made the event socket's task panic (`ZoneEvent::Error` could not be serialized), so the web UI lost its connection and showed nothing. The event is now `{"type":"error","message":…}` inside `zone_event`, an event that does not serialize is logged and skipped instead of closing the socket, and a test serializes every zone event. The web UI shows the error in the player bar and on the zone's card in Zones, and says it once as a toast.
- **A BluOS zone no longer dies on play.** Opening a BluOS player from the zone's audio thread called `spawn_blocking` outside the Tokio runtime and panicked, which left the zone dead. Zones now open their outputs inside the node's runtime (the same applied to MPD, Sonos and HEOS groups), and a panic in a zone's thread no longer kills it: the zone stops, reports the panic as its error, and plays again on the next play.
- **AirPlay 2 speakers that refused the stream play (Bluesound and others), and a Linn's AirPlay survives a pause.** A receiver that lists MFi authentication (`et` 4 in its `_raop._tcp` record) answered ANNOUNCE with 403: the node now sends `POST /auth-setup` first (a public key; nothing is checked and the stream stays unencrypted), which the Bluesound PULSE FLEX 2i accepts. A receiver that still refuses says so as the zone's error ("this AirPlay speaker refused the stream (403): it wants AirPlay 2 pairing, which the node does not do yet"). A receiver that hangs up (a Linn closes a quiet RTSP connection; pausing then failed with `FLUSH: bad answer ""`) is now "the receiver closed the connection", a flush on it is not an error, and the next write opens a new session. While playing, the node sends an RTSP OPTIONS every 25 s so the connection stays open.
- **A BluOS player (and any renderer that buffers fast) hears the whole queue.** A pull stream went out as fast as the renderer took it: a BluOS player took 40 s of audio in a second, the zone reached "the end" and stopped it after a second of sound. Pull sinks now keep to real time (at most 5 s ahead), and at the end of the queue the zone waits for the renderer to play what it has before it stops it. Squeezebox players keep their own pacing.
- **AirPlay 2 receivers get their time during the handshake.** The node answers the receiver's timing requests from the start of the session, not after RECORD: an AirPlay 2 speaker asks during SETUP and RECORD and waited for it (the zone failed with "Resource temporarily unavailable"). A request the receiver does not answer now says which one.
- **Scans skip a Mac's `._` files.** The AppleDouble resource forks a Mac leaves next to each file on a share (`._song.flac`) were read as audio, each one failed, and they filled the scan's errors.
- **MusicBrainz is asked one request at a time for the whole node.** Each part of the node (identify, discovery, artist pages, new releases, the `/mb` proxy) kept its own one-a-second pace, so together they went over MusicBrainz's limit and got 503s (seen during a big Identify on the owner's NUC). One gate per MusicBrainz server now spaces every request a second apart and runs them one at a time, and a 503's Retry-After holds back all of them. `musicbrainz_base_url` in the config (`PM_MUSICBRAINZ_URL`) points the node, and its proxy, at a mirror.
- **New releases asked MusicBrainz's website, not its API** (`/release-group` without `/ws/2`), so it never had an answer; it asks `/ws/2/release-group` now.
- **A port in use no longer restarts the node in a loop without saying why.** The API port is taken first; when it is in use the node stops at once with "cannot listen on 0.0.0.0:7788: the port is already in use, most likely by another Pubky Music Node on this machine…". A busy Squeezebox port (3483) only turns Squeezebox players off, with a warning naming the port.
- **A scan no longer skips files with "database is locked"** (and saving a queue during a scan no longer fails with it). During a large first scan of two shares, files were skipped whenever another connection (the other scan, the API) had written since the batch began: the scan batch read first and asked for the write lock at its first write, which SQLite refuses at once in WAL mode. Batches now take the write lock when they start (`BEGIN IMMEDIATE`) and wait for other writers, who in turn get the lock between batches (a waiting connection now tries every millisecond for up to 10 s, where SQLite's own timeout slept up to 100 ms between tries and lost the race to a busy scan); a file still turned away by a busy database is tried again after the walk, before files not seen count as removed. A rescan picks up the files skipped before (they are new to the library).
- **Symphonia's warnings about harmless things in files** (junk bytes before a frame, ID3 frames it skips, the MP3 bit reservoir) no longer show at the default log level; `RUST_LOG` that names `symphonia` shows them.
- **Covers say where they play from.** A small badge in the bottom corner of album and song covers (tiles, song rows with a cover, the album page, the player bar and the Now playing cover) shows the service or the library: the service's glyph in its own colour on a dark disc with a light ring, so it reads on light and dark covers, named for screen readers ("From TIDAL"). The badge sits over the cover, so nothing moves. Home's tiles keep theirs, now named the same way; the item page's source line and the playlist source chips stay.

Move to says "Moving to …" the same way.
- **Move playback to another device from the Play to menu.** When something plays or is queued, every other device in the menu gets **Move here** (and This zone gets **Move to…**): the song at its position, the queue, repeat and radio move there, paused stays paused, and the menu follows. Errors from the node (a zone not open to listeners, an empty queue) show in the menu. With the node on the same computer the player listens on loopback only (before, it was not registered at all), and it listens again elsewhere when the node changes. Endpoints are no longer advertised over mDNS or read from `[outputs] endpoints` (a warning when it is set): `pm-endpoint` is the app's player only, and the headless binary, for tests, needs `--key-file` or `--key`. A Raspberry Pi next to a DAC runs squeezelite (`squeezelite -n Kitchen -s <node-ip>`), which the docs and Settings › Speakers and outputs now say. The release downloads no longer include `pm-endpoint`; it still builds for tests.
- **The zones answer as renderers on the LAN only** (security review 2026-10-27, high 2). The UPnP/DLNA routes take no token, like any renderer, so the node now serves them only to a private or loopback source address on its own listener: never through remote access (iroh gets its own listener, so the node knows), the Cloudflare tunnel or a proxy (any forwarding header), which get 403. The URIs a controller sets (`SetAVTransportURI`, `SetNextAVTransportURI`, which now also needs http(s)) and its GENA callbacks must resolve to LAN or public addresses: loopback only for a controller on the same machine, never link-local (cloud metadata), unspecified or multicast. A node without an API token answers only directly, and its remote access stays off.
- **A listener sees and plays only the zones open to listeners, everywhere** (security review 2026-10-27, medium 5). A transfer's target zone now needs the same right as its source (it was not checked). `GET /v1/zones` and the event socket's snapshot listed every zone to every caller; a listener now gets only theirs, and an event about another zone arrives as `{"seq","type":"hidden"}`, so `seq` stays without gaps and clients do not resync.
- **Streams never read a whole file, and transcodes are bounded** (security review 2026-10-27, medium 8 and 9). The browser stream and Subsonic `stream`/`download` read local files in 256 KiB chunks from where the range starts; a file on a share or drive is fetched once, in chunks, into the download cache (stopped when the source is silent 30 s or the file is bigger than the cache) and every seek comes from there. ffmpeg transcodes run at most one per CPU (a request waits up to 60 s for a slot, then 503) within a time limit (a minute plus a quarter of the track, at most 3 h; `downloads.transcode_timeout_s`), past which ffmpeg is killed and the answer is 504. The per-file lock map no longer grows.
- **An endpoint cannot be held by a silent peer** (security review 2026-10-27, medium 6, first part). `pm-endpoint` (and the apps' built-in player) closes a connection that does not say Hello within 10 s or stays silent 60 s before opening an output; an open output (a paused zone) keeps no read timeout but TCP keepalive; writes time out after 30 s. At most 16 sessions at once. Audio frames are at most 4 MiB (was 32) and control frames 256 KiB; the node sends a larger block in several frames.
- **Three small leaks closed** (security review 2026-10-27, lows). A listener's `/v1/info` keeps the last scan's `error_count` but not the unreadable files' paths (where the owner keeps music). The Pubky session secret (Ring sign-in and `pm-node login`) and the secrets a backup restores are created 0600 as a side file and renamed into place, never written first and made private after, and an old file readable by others is replaced. `DELETE /v1/me/token` reads the token the way the API does, so a listener signed in with `?token=` signs out too.
- **Security: the API token no longer goes to whoever claims your node's id from another address** (security review 2026-10-27, #3 and #4). The id is announced in the clear on the LAN, so anyone could claim it.
  - **The app:** when the remembered node doesn't answer and a node with its id answers at another address, the app tries the remote tunnel first (it reaches the node by its key, so it proves the node). Otherwise the connect page asks: "Use http://… ?" The token goes there only on yes, and the node there must have the same id.
  - **Home Assistant:** a zeroconf announcement of a set-up node from another address no longer moves the entry and sends its token there. It shows a "node at a new address?" card; on yes it checks the node there and moves the entry only if the id matches. A node that is connected, or at the same address, is left alone.
  - **Home Assistant cover images:** whether an image is on the node is decided by parsing the URL and comparing scheme, host and port. The prefix check gave the token to a URL whose user info was the node's address (so its host was another one), to `http://node:7788.attacker.example/…` and to `http://node:77880/…`.
- **Security: links from data can't run script in the node's page** (security review 2026-10-27, #7). A MusicBrainz homepage, a radio station's site or a pasted link could be a `javascript:` URL, and the page rendered it as a link.
  - **On the node:** every About fact's link goes out only when it is http or https (one check, applied when a fact is sent, however it was built), and a MusicBrainz homepage is kept only when it is one.
  - **In the page:** every link and "open" taken from data goes through the same check; one that fails shows as plain text. Pubky Ring links keep their own scheme.
  - **Content-Security-Policy** on the node's pages (`index.html`, `party.html`): scripts run only from the node and the pages' own inline scripts, by hash, so a `javascript:` link or an injected script does nothing. No plugins, and `<base>` only to the node. Fetches, images and media stay open (covers and streams come from everywhere). The inline `onerror` handlers on images became one listener, so the policy needs no `unsafe-inline`.
  - **The app's settings file** (it holds the node's token) is created readable only by you under a side name and renamed into place. Before, it was written and then made private, so for a moment anyone could open it, and whoever did kept reading through that handle. A crash mid-write now leaves the old file.
- **Speakers get an address they can reach without `public_url`.** With `[api] public_url` (`PM_PUBLIC_URL`) empty and the node listening on every interface (`0.0.0.0`, `[::]`, as in Docker and on Umbrel), the node used `http://0.0.0.0:7788`, which no speaker can fetch from. It now uses this machine's address on its way to the network with the listen port, and says which in the log; a loopback `listen` keeps `127.0.0.1`. Sign-in callbacks (TIDAL, Spotify, Jamendo, Last.fm, the drives) still follow the address the browser used when `public_url` is not set, and Settings' network check says when the picked address looks like a VPN or Docker's bridge. The Docker, TrueNAS and Linux setups no longer need `PM_PUBLIC_URL`.
- **The node download archive carries the guides.** README.md, docs/deploy.md and docs/guides/ sit at their repo paths in the archive, so the README's links to the guides work in the unpacked folder (CI checks the links between them).

Writes still go through when the device list is unreadable (the node's own record applies), and `GET /v1/vault` shows the problem. On homeservers without locks, sign out everywhere takes the lease and a refresh can't bring a deleted sign-in back.
  - An old key of the node under the same grant signs out only that key, not the node. Sign out everywhere deletes each sign-in under its lock and lists any left. One unreadable sign-in no longer takes `GET /v1/vault` down, and "sign in again" answers 409.
  - Revoking tokens at the services comes with each source's vault integration, and the answer says so.
  - The screens are mn-app's.
- **Vault refreshes don't race** (STEWARD-VAULT.md item 4). `Vault::refresh` takes the homeserver's WebDAV lock on the sign-in (30 s, renewed every 10 s while the service answers) and reads it again. If another client already refreshed it, it uses that; otherwise it refreshes the token it just read and writes it back with `last_refresh`.
  - Where locks are refused it uses the lease file instead.
  - A refused refresh is never written over the vault.
  - New tokens are never dropped: a failed write is tried again, taking the lock again if it lapsed, and otherwise handed back to the caller. A write made meanwhile that changes no tokens (a new device) is kept. The lease is renewed like the lock, and the service gets at most 60 s.
  - Tested on a real homeserver against a fake service that rotates refresh tokens: two clients refresh at once, under the lock and with the lease file, and the service is asked once. A 33-second refresh keeps its lock and its lease (another client at 31 s still waits). A refresh that can't be saved hands its tokens back.
- **Vault devices prove themselves** (STEWARD-VAULT.md item 3). Only a grant that names the vault itself (`/priv/pubky.music/vault/:rw`) counts: an app approved for all storage is not trusted with the sign-ins. The node asks Ring for that capability, and an older sign-in is asked to sign in again. Each client registers in `/priv/pubky.music/vault/index.json` with the pubky SDK's custom grant proof: Ring's grant, plus a proof over its vault key signed with the grant's client key. Apps never hold the identity secret.
  - Only entries the SDK's verifier accepts get keys: the owner's grant, covering the vault, for that key, and not signed out.
  - A new device is added to existing sign-ins by one that can read them (same epoch). A signed-out device can't re-register, and the next write rotates past it.
  - Each client remembers every sign-out it has seen in a local file, because the device list is writable by the devices it lists. A signed-out device that writes the list back without its record stays signed out, and the next registration writes the record back. The node's own sign-outs are never dropped from that file, records from the list are bounded and checked, and records about the node itself aren't kept, so a flooded or hostile list can't make it trust a signed-out device or grow the list past its limit. Who gets a key is read under each blob's lock.
  - Untrusted entries can't fill the device list: a full list drops them to make room, and a list with more than 64 entries is still read.
  - Vault files are written under the homeserver's WebDAV lock.
  - Tested on a real homeserver with forged, borrowed and garbage entries.
- **Quieter source switching, working services first** (STEWARD-UX-PLAYER-SOURCES.md §1):
  - On song, album and artist pages the source row is now one small line under the title, not a row of pills: "From Your library · also Jamendo, TIDAL, Deezer", then "2 more".
  - The order: the page's own source first; then services that are switched on and working, in the owner's play order; then the rest behind "more". Services switched off don't appear.
  - Sources and a playlist song's "Play this song from" menu use the same order.
- **The node reads and writes the sources vault** (STEWARD-VAULT.md item 2, `pm_pubky::vault`).
  - The node gets its own X25519 vault key: a 0600 file, made once, equal to the Noise static key pubky-noise derives from the same secret.
  - `Vault` reads and writes a person's sign-ins over their session, encrypted to the node and to the clients the caller names.
  - Rewriting keeps the data key unless a client is dropped; then the key rotates. A sign-in the node can't open is never overwritten.
  - Tested against a real homeserver 0.16.2 in CI's bundle job: two clients share a sign-in, the homeserver holds only ciphertext, and a dropped client can't read the next write. Nothing in the UI uses it yet.
- **Downloads for phones and laptops** (STEWARD-DOWNLOADS.md): `GET /v1/library/tracks/{id}/download` serves the original, or a transcode with `?format=aac|mp3|opus&bitrate=`, and lets an interrupted download resume (ranges, `If-Range`, `ETag`).

- **The sources vault, spec v1** (STEWARD-VAULT.md item 1): `crates/pubky-music-specs/VAULT.md`. Streaming sign-ins go to `/priv/pubky.music/vault/<service>.json`, encrypted on the device to every client the person authorised. The building blocks:
